Is Differential Radial Flow Collective?

Role of multiplicity fluctuations and non-flow bias

Aman Dimri, Somadutta Bhatta, and Jiangyong Jia

Quark Matter 2025
Frankfurt am Main, Germany · April 2025

Poster PDF Related Project

Overview

This poster presented a differential view of radial-flow fluctuations using the observable \(v_0(p_T)\). While earlier work had already established the collective nature of radial flow in Pb+Pb collisions, this poster focused on two specific issues that are essential for interpreting the measurement: the role of non-flow contamination and the impact of multiplicity fluctuations.

The goal was to understand which observed features of the differential observable arise from genuine collective dynamics and which may instead reflect biases from particle production or short-range correlations.

The Differential Observable

Radial flow is commonly quantified through event-by-event fluctuations of the mean transverse momentum, \([p_T]\). A differential version of this idea probes how fluctuations in the local particle spectrum at a given \(p_T\) are correlated with fluctuations in the event-wise radial-flow signal.

The poster discussed two related formulations of differential radial flow:

  • \(v_0(p_T)\), based on the normalized spectrum \(n(p_T)\),
  • \(v_0'(p_T)\), based on the full spectrum \(N(p_T)\).

These two constructions are closely related, but they do not respond in the same way to multiplicity fluctuations. Their difference therefore provides a useful handle on the underlying particle-production dynamics.

What the Poster Examined

The poster focused on two physics questions.

First, it examined whether characteristic features seen in the measured \(v_0(p_T)\) could be influenced by non-flow effects, especially at high \(p_T\). For this purpose, HIJING was used as a non-collective baseline model.

Second, it studied how global multiplicity fluctuations modify the differential observable, and how the comparison between \(v_0(p_T)\) and \(v_0'(p_T)\) can be used to isolate that contribution.

Impact of Non-Flow Bias

A central point of the poster was that the behavior of \(v_0(p_T)\) in HIJING differs qualitatively from the collective expectation.

In particular:

  • the factorization behavior is broken in HIJING,
  • the result depends on the choice of the reference \(p_T\) range,
  • and \(v_0(p_T)\) rises approximately linearly at high \(p_T\).

These observations support the interpretation that some of the splitting behavior and the strong rise at large \(p_T\) seen in peripheral data are likely influenced by non-flow contamination rather than pure collective radial flow.

Impact of Multiplicity Fluctuations

The second major message was that \(v_0(p_T)\) itself is comparatively stable across different event ensembles, while \(v_0'(p_T)\) is more sensitive to multiplicity fluctuations.

This makes the difference

\[ v_0'(p_T) - v_0(p_T) \]

a useful diagnostic quantity. The poster showed that this difference is approximately constant in \(p_T\), consistent with the expectation that it isolates the contribution from multiplicity fluctuations.

This provides a new way to constrain fluctuations in the particle-production process, especially when varying the event classifier or rapidity interval.

Main Takeaways

The poster highlighted two main conclusions:

  • some features of the measured differential radial-flow signal, especially the splitting behavior and the large-\(p_T\) rise in peripheral events, can be attributed to non-flow contamination;
  • the difference between the two formulations of differential radial flow can be used to isolate and understand multiplicity fluctuations.

Together, these results sharpen the interpretation of \(v_0(p_T)\) and clarify how this observable can be used as a robust probe of collective dynamics.

Relation to the Publication

This poster should be viewed as a companion presentation to the project

“The shape of differential radial flow \(v_0(p_T)\), not its zero-crossing, carries physical information”
Nuclear Science and Techniques 37, 163 (2026).

While the publication develops the main physical interpretation of the differential radial-flow observable, this poster emphasized two additional practical aspects: how to identify non-flow contributions and how to use the difference between alternative definitions to probe multiplicity fluctuations.
